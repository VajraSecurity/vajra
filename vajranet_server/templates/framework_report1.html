A. About Vajra

Vajra is an open-source Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) tool. It uses an enhanced Osquery framework to provide deep endpoint visibility and telemetry. Its rich ruleset enables detection of malware in real time and allows security teams to take immediate, responsible action.

Provenance of the evidence. The figures in this report rest on a mixed evidence base and are not uniformly agent-verified. Three kinds of evidence are combined without being distinguished per control: telemetry collected from live Vajra agents and stored in the Vajra compliance database; platform-capability assertions, where a control records that Vajra collects, monitors or reports a class of data rather than that the endpoint is configured securely — the majority of the SEBI CSCRF and IRDAI control points are of this kind; and operator-attested answers, where a CERT-In guideline has no automated check and an administrator's Yes/No response is recorded against every endpoint in scope. A small number of controls are additionally evaluated from platform records rather than from an agent sweep. This report should therefore not be read as an assertion that every figure below is machine-verified, nor that none of it is self-assessed.

This report presents the organization's compliance posture against the <%= framework_name %> framework (<%= framework_subtitle %>), covering <%= total_checks %> control check points across the <%= total_nodes %> Windows endpoints counted by the latest sweep (<%= assessed_basis %>). The edition, revision or circular of <%= framework_name %> from which these check points were derived is not recorded in the compliance snapshot, and is therefore not stated in this report.

D. Non-Compliant Endpoint Inventory

The table below lists every endpoint that failed at least one compliance control in the most recent sweep, along with its system metadata. Endpoints are sorted by compliance score ascending so the most critical gaps appear first. The Not Assessed column counts the controls that could be neither passed nor failed on that endpoint; those controls are excluded from the score rather than counted against it, and a dash means the snapshot does not record the figure.

Some snapshots record only which endpoints were non-compliant, without recording which controls they failed. Those rows are listed with the endpoint identified and the score column left blank: their score was never stored and is not reconstructed here. A blank score is not a pass, a failure, or a score of zero.

Where a snapshot records the controls an endpoint failed and the controls that went unassessed on it, the unassessed ones are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator of its score and counted in the Not Assessed column. Where it records only the failed controls, the number of unassessed controls is unknown rather than zero: the score shown is then the share of all <%= total_checks %> controls the endpoint is not recorded as failing, which counts any control it was never measured on as though it had passed. Such a score is an upper bound and is printed as ≤ n% with a dash in the Not Assessed column; the endpoint's true score cannot be higher, and may be lower.

The table below lists every evaluated control with its pass rate, host counts, and the corresponding standard reference clause for direct audit traceability. Pass rates of 90 % or above are marked in green; amber indicates 75–89 %; red indicates below 75 %. A grey N/A means no pass rate was recorded for that control in this snapshot, and a grey Not assessed means the snapshot records neither a pass nor a fail for that control on a single host, so there is nothing to compute a rate from - in neither case is the control a pass or a fail, and neither is counted as a failure anywhere in this report or in the charts above. The Not Assessed column counts, out of the <%= total_nodes %> endpoints this sweep covered, the hosts on which the control was neither passed nor failed; those hosts are excluded from the pass rate rather than counted against it, and a dash means the snapshot does not record the endpoint population to measure the shortfall against. A control badged Not assessed therefore shows the full swept population in this column, not a zero: nothing was measured on any of them.

Controls marked Attested have no automated check in Vajra. Their result is an administrator's Yes/No answer recorded against every endpoint in scope, not a measured per-host outcome, and no pass rate is printed for them.
